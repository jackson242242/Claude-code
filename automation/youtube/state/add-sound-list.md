### How to use these sounds for the POV feel (applies to every sound above)
- Prepare a **version with no music bed**: voice and subtitles only, no track from `assets/music/`. Otherwise
  the trending sound plays on top of our own music. In the app, set the original audio low (about 0-20%) and
  the added sound high.
- Cut on the beat: #4, #5 and #10 need fast cuts. #1, #2, #3, #6 and #13 need long, steady walking shots.
- On TikTok and Instagram, **business accounts** only get the commercial or royalty-free library, so most
  label songs (#4-#8, #10) will be missing. #1 is reported as business-cleared.

## Can a Shorts-library sound be added to a Short that is ALREADY uploaded?

**Short answer: none of the official YouTube pages I found says yes. Plan as if it is NO for trending label
songs, and add the sound when the Short is created in the YouTube app.**

What the official YouTube Help pages say (based on search summaries):
- **Add an audio track to your video**, https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/94316 . The YouTube Studio
  **Editor on a computer** (Content > video > Editor > Audio > Save) can add music to an uploaded video from
  YouTube's library. It also says the **mobile app can no longer change an uploaded video's audio track**. The
  page does not say that the label-licensed Shorts library is available there. Third-party guides
  (soundstripe.com, jupitrr.com, 2026) say this Studio option offers **YouTube Audio Library tracks only**.
  Those are royalty-free tracks, not the trending songs. One guide adds that edits can be blocked after 100K
  views for non-YPP channels.
- **Shorts music** comes from record labels that "have signed Shorts specific licensing agreements with
  YouTube". Depending on the song, you can use **up to 90 seconds of music in a 3-minute Short "when using
  YouTube tools"**, meaning the in-app Shorts creation flow:
  https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13053317 (Manage Shorts as a rights holder),
  https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13486873 (Music eligibility for YouTube Shorts)
- The Shorts creation tools, including adding sound, are documented only for the creation flow, during and
  right after recording or importing: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12948448 ,
  https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12779649 (Upload Shorts, iPhone/iPad) and
  https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13521394 (Add & edit audio, Android)
- **"Use this sound" / Remix**: the official way to reuse a sound from someone else's Short. It makes a **new**
  Short; it does not change an existing one: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10623810

**Practical workflow for the owner:** for each Short that should get a trending sound, the daily routine
should **not** upload it through the API. It should leave a music-free MP4 for the owner. The owner then
imports that MP4 from the phone gallery in the YouTube app (+ > Short > upload), taps **Add sound**, searches
the term in the table, turns the original audio down, and publishes. If the Short has already been
API-uploaded, the only post-upload option is the Studio desktop Editor's royalty-free library.

### Monetization and Content ID
I could not re-check these with an official page this session because the search budget ran out. The
following is from my training knowledge and is **unverified this session**. Check it here:
https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220 (YouTube Shorts monetization policies).
- A Short that uses a licensed Shorts-library track can still earn Shorts revenue share. However, part of
  that Short's slice of the Creator Pool goes to the music rights holders: about half for one track, more for
  two. A sound added through YouTube's own tools is the licensed route. It is not treated like uploading the
  same song inside your MP4, which Content ID can claim and, for Shorts longer than 1 minute with an active
  claim, block.
- **For this channel today it does not matter financially.** With 71 subscribers the channel is not in YPP,
  so there is no Shorts revenue to split. The real risks are (a) a Content ID claim if a song ever ends up
  baked into the uploaded file, and (b) going past the per-song length limit, so keep sound-backed Shorts at
  60 s or less.
