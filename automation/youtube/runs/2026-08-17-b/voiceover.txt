For about a century, more than 60 million pieces of Chinese porcelain sailed to Europe — and nearly all of it passed through one city. Before 1842, Guangzhou was China's only legal export port. Plain white porcelain was made inland at Jingdezhen, then shipped to Canton, where local artists repainted it in gold and rose. This is Guangcai — Canton porcelain. From the 1740s, European nobles mailed in their own designs — family crests, portraits — and Canton artisans copied them onto the glaze. A custom hand-painted teaset, shipped by sea. Expert tip: look for the dense gold rim to tell real Canton ware. One port, sixty million pieces — worth a look?
