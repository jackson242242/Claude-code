## Verified facts + sources (this run)
- Yum cha (饮茶, "drink tea") is the Cantonese morning-tea ritual; "一盅两件" = one
  pot of tea + two dim sum plates — [Journal of Ethnic Foods (Springer)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s42779-023-00180-9) · [Wikipedia — Yum cha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yum_cha)
- Roots in Qing-dynasty Guangzhou: the "one-port" trade policy funneled foreign
  commerce through Canton, teahouses flourished — [China Explorer Tour](https://chinaexplorertour.com/2024/blog/how-to-enjoy-cantonese-morning-tea-a-delicious-tradition/) · [newsgd.com](https://info.newsgd.com/node_70feb3a062/25cf8f10bd.shtml)
- Finger-tapping = silent thank-you when tea is poured, mimicking a kowtow; tied to
  a Qianlong-era legend — [NextShark](https://nextshark.com/people-always-tap-two-fingers-table-eating-dim-sum) · [Golden Moon Tea](https://www.goldenmoontea.com/blogs/tea/106695239-why-the-chinese-tap-2-fingers-with-every-pour-of-tea)
- Guangzhou yum cha culture named an Intangible Cultural Heritage of Guangzhou (2007)
  — [Wikipedia — Yum cha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yum_cha)
