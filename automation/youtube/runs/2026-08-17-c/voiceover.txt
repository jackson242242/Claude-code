Almost every old Chinese city was ringed in a stone wall. This 800-year-old town in Yunnan never built one — on purpose. The ruling Naxi chieftains kept it open to traders. One local tale: their surname meant "wood," and a frame around it spells "trapped." What holds the town together is water — snowmelt runs through every lane in stone canals, with a one-well, three-pool system for drinking, washing food, and laundry. Over 300 old bridges cross the streams, and the Naxi still write in living pictographs. Expert tip: wander before 8am, before the tour flags arrive. A town with no walls, alive 800 years — would you wander in?
