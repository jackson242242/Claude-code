## Verified facts (WebSearch this run)
- Jiayu Pass (Jiayuguan) is the first/westernmost frontier fortress at the west end of
  the Ming-dynasty Great Wall, by Jiayuguan city, Gansu, on the edge of the Gobi.
- Begun in 1372 (Hongwu yr 5) by the Ming officer Feng Sheng — nine years earlier than
  Shanhaiguan at the eastern end; the whole system took ~168 years (1372-1540).
- "天下第一雄关" — "the First and Greatest Pass Under Heaven" — was inscribed on a tablet
  in 1873 by Zuo Zongtang (then governing the northwest campaign).
- Unlike the grey-brick/stone sections near Beijing, the Jiayuguan wall is built mainly
  of loess, tamped in layers — very solid in the dry Gobi climate.
- West of the fort's westernmost abutment ("First Pier", built 1539) stretches the vast Gobi.
- EP7 tease: the Great Wall's beacon-tower (烽火台) signal relay could carry a warning over
  great distances quickly — a verifiable heritage subject for the next episode.
