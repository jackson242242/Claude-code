In a village in Shandong, a farmer sells a bowl of hand-pulled noodles. The price? Three yuan. That's about forty cents.

He kept it at three yuan for fifteen years. People told him to charge more. He wouldn't — his neighbors, he said, couldn't afford it.

Then one winter, a stranger's video made him famous overnight. Crowds came. Livestreamers came. A few even pretended to be his relatives.

It got so loud he slipped off to a friend's house just to sleep. But he used the attention for something kind — helping a father search for his stolen son. They found him.

The noodles? Still three yuan.

So, honest question: keep the price, or finally cash in?
