China has the world's biggest high-speed rail network — trains at 350 kilometres an hour. But in the mountains of Sichuan, one train still crawls.

It's train 5633. The cheapest ticket is two yuan — about thirty US cents — and it hasn't changed in decades.

To the villagers along the line, many of them Yi people, it isn't really a train. It's a moving market: they board with chickens, ducks and baskets of vegetables, and sell them down the valley. There's even a car just for livestock.

The bullet trains skipped these villages, so the country keeps the slow one running — on purpose, as a lifeline.

So, honest question: in an age of 350-kilometre trains, would you keep the thirty-cent one — or let it go?
