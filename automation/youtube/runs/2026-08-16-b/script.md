## Verified facts (sources found this run)
- Longquan celadon (Zhejiang). History traces back to ~220 AD (Three Kingdoms); reached its golden era in the Southern Song (1127–1279), when the celebrated plum-green (梅子青) and pale/misty-blue (粉青) glazes were achieved.
- On 30 September 2009, the traditional firing techniques of Longquan celadon were inscribed on UNESCO's Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity — the first and only ceramic in the world on that list.
- Glaze is compounded from violet-golden clay + a mix of burnt feldspar, limestone, quartz and plant ash; recipes are handed down within families/across generations. The piece is fired in repeated cycles of heating and cooling, with temperatures reaching as high as ~1310 °C.
- The prized effect is a jade-like green ("green as jade").
