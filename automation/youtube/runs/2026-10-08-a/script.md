## The overturned belief
Travellers picture the cormorant fisherman on the back of China's ¥20 note as a working
fisherman. The famous ones (the Huang brothers at Xingping, Yellow Cloth Shoal) gave up
commercial fishing around 2008 and now work as professional photo models; the cormorant
"fishing" visitors photograph at dawn/sunset is largely a staged performance. Overfishing
and ecology made posing for photographers pay far better than fishing. (Some elderly
masters may still demonstrate the craft; reports from 2013/2021 conflict on whether any
still fish for a living — so the honest claim is "the famous ¥20-note fishermen quit in
2008 and pose for photos," not "nobody fishes.")

## Verified facts (sources in description)
- The ¥20 banknote reverse shows a Li River cormorant fisherman with Yellow Cloth Shoal
  behind him, just downstream of Xingping. (China Daily)
- The best-known fishermen (Huang Quande & Huang Yuechuang) gave up fishing in 2008 and
  work as professional models; visitors photograph them almost daily. (China Daily 2016)
- A documenting photographer: overfishing/ecology meant the men could earn "quite a bit
  more posing for photographers." (China Digital Times 2013)
