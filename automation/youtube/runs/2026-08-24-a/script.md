## Verified facts (sourced this run)
- One cocoon = one continuous filament, ~600–900 m long; several are reeled together
  into a thread. — ChinaHighlights, Silk Road Foundation.
- China kept sericulture a state secret for ~1,000+ years after silk began moving west
  (~400 BC); smuggling silkworm eggs/cocoons/mulberry seeds was punishable by death. — ChinaHighlights.
- Two Persian/Byzantine monks smuggled silkworm eggs west in hollow canes/walking sticks
  c. 550 AD for Emperor Justinian. — ChinaHighlights, Britannica.
- The trade routes carrying it were named the "Silk Road." — Britannica.
- "Sericulture and silk craftsmanship of China" inscribed on UNESCO Representative List
  of Intangible Cultural Heritage in 2009. — CGTN, China.org.cn, UNESCO ICH.
- Real vs fake silk burn test: real silk smells like burnt hair, self-extinguishes,
  curls to brittle black ash you can crush; polyester melts into a hard plastic bead
  with a chemical smell. — ElleSilk, Sartor Bohemia burn-test guides.
