## Hook strategy
Fact-first + reversal: a *vassal* king cast a **gold** seal reading "Emperor" (文帝行玺),
breaking the Qin-Han white-jade rule. Twist: the same tomb held a **Persian** silver box —
a 2,000-year-old imported "haul." Expert Tip second-to-last; either-or question last
(homemade emperor vs Silk-Road trader).

## Verified facts + sources (checked 2026-08-10)
- **文帝行玺 gold seal:** largest Western Han gold seal found to date; **dragon knob**;
  3.1×3×1.8 cm, **148.5 g**, ~**98% gold**. Qin-Han imperial seals were white jade with
  tiger/chimaera knobs — casting one in gold with a dragon was a deliberate break. The
  second Nanyue king styled himself 文帝 ("Emperor Wen"), though Nanyue was a Han vassal.
- **Persian silver box (蒜头纹银盒):** flattened sphere, garlic-bulb/petal repoussé on lid
  and body; **chasing (锤揲) technique**, West-Asian/Persian metalware style → experts judge
  it **imported** (maritime trade). After reaching China, craftsmen **welded a knob** to the
  lid and **added a bronze ring foot**; used as a **medicine box**, held pills when found.
- Sources: 南越王博物院 (nywmuseum.org.cn); 文帝行玺金印 & 南越文王墓 (zh.wikipedia.org).
