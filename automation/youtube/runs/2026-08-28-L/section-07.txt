Number four — the one I promised you at the very start, the mistake that gets people turned around after they've already crossed an ocean. On the transit system, your onward ticket has to go to a different country or region than the one you arrived from. Read that twice. Fly in from Tokyo, and you cannot simply book a flight back to Tokyo — that's not transit, that's a round trip, and it breaks the rule instantly. You need a genuine third destination. This is where people get clever and get burned: they book China and back, assume it counts, and find out at the border that it doesn't. Now, one very useful piece of good news is hiding in this rule. For transit purposes, Hong Kong, Macau, and Taiwan count as separate regions — so a mainland-China-to-Hong-Kong onward ticket can absolutely qualify as that third destination. Get the direction wrong, though, and no amount of arguing at the desk will save you; the officer isn't being difficult, the rule is simply binary. So map your route as A, to China, to C — never A, to China, and back to A.
