Number seven: payments, and this one is free, but skip it and your whole trip gets harder. China runs on two apps: Alipay and WeChat Pay. Both now let foreign travelers link an overseas Visa or Mastercard directly, and Alipay's built-in Tour Pass is designed for short visits. Cash still works, but many stalls and taxis really expect a QR code. Set both apps up at home, link your card, and you'll pay for everything, from noodles to the metro to train tickets, by scanning your phone. Number two, and this is the fifty-to-a-hundred-dollar mistake. China's high-speed trains are magnificent, and they're priced by distance: roughly half a yuan per kilometer in second class. A Beijing-to-Tianjin hop is about thirty minutes and twenty-six to fifty-five yuan, four to eight dollars. But a long haul like Beijing to Shanghai is thirteen hundred kilometers, and that's where the class matters: second class is around five hundred fifty yuan, while business class can be three times that. The trap isn't the train, it's booking business or first class just because it's a bullet train. Second class is spacious, quiet, and just as fast. Book it on Trip.com or the official twelve-three-oh-six site with your passport, and pocket the difference. Which leaves the one cost you actually control the most.
