China Travel Expert 🧭 | Your fine teacup and a whole country share one word — "china."

True porcelain is fired near 1,300°C until clay vitrifies into a glass-hard, translucent body. Its secret is kaolin — "china clay" — named after Gaoling village beside Jingdezhen, which fed the imperial kilns for 1,000+ years. Ming voyages carried its blue-and-white across the seas until the ware became the country's name.

Expert Tip: tap the rim — real porcelain rings like a bell; a dull thud means a hairline crack.
