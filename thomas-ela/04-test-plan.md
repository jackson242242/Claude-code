# 04 · Test Plan — Administration, Scoring & Progress Monitoring

How to run the diagnostic fairly, turn raw scores into a profile, and re-test so you can
*see* growth over time.

---

## 1. Purpose & principles

- **Diagnostic, not graded.** The point is to find *where to focus*, not to rank Thomas.
  Tell him this up front so he attempts the hard items instead of leaving them blank.
- **Find the ceiling.** Grade-5 stretch items are included so a strong reader (82nd %ile)
  doesn't max out. We want to see where he *starts* to struggle.
- **Separate skill from speed.** Comprehension is scored on correctness; fluency/stamina
  is observed, not timed-to-failure.

---

## 2. Administration

| Item | Guidance |
|------|----------|
| **Setting** | Quiet table, no phone, water nearby. Morning is ideal for focus. |
| **Total time** | ~80 min. Not a race — allow up to 95 if he's still working productively. The missions are meatier than worksheet items, so this is *thinking* time. |
| **Breaks** | Let him break between any two missions — they're self-contained. |
| **What he may do** | Re-read, underline, jot plans (Missions 2 & 4 reward planning), cross out, change his mind. |
| **What to avoid** | Don't hint at answers, define vocabulary, or tell him "the hamster did it." The struggle *is* the data. |
| **Read-aloud?** | Don't read passages to him (that changes what's measured). You *may* re-explain a *mission's job* if he's unsure what's being asked. |
| **Mindset to set** | "These are real jobs, not quizzes. Take risks on the hard parts — a smart-but-wrong attempt tells us more than a blank." |

### Observing Fluency & Stamina (the un-scored domain)

While he reads any mission's text, optionally have him read **one paragraph aloud** and note:
- Did he read smoothly with expression, or word-by-word?
- Did focus fade in the longer Part 2 passage (a stamina signal)?
- Did he go back and re-read to understand (good strategy) or guess?

Rate 1–4 and record it as the Fluency/Stamina domain score (×25% → a %).

## 3. Scoring workflow

1. Score all multiple-choice/short items with the **key** (file 03 §A).
2. Score constructed items with the **rubrics** (file 03 §B). When unsure between two
   scores, pick the lower and note why — honest data helps Thomas more than inflated data.
3. Fill the **per-domain table** (file 03 §C): sum points per domain ÷ max = **% mastery**.
4. Record everything in the **tracking table** below.

### Worked example (illustrative only — not Thomas's real scores)

> Suppose he scores: Inference & Evidence 4/6 (67%), Constructed Response 6/10 (60%),
> Informational 6/7 (86%), Vocabulary 7/8 (88%), Literature 7/8 (88%), Conventions 8/8
> (100%), Fluency 3/4 (75%).
>
> **Interpretation:** strengths exactly where predicted (conventions, vocab, literature);
> the two top-weighted domains — *constructed response* and *inference/evidence* — land in
> the "developing" band. → The diagnosis's pre-test bet is **confirmed**, and the study
> plan front-loads writing-with-evidence. This is the *expected* pattern for a strong-MAP
> reader, and it's exactly the actionable insight the single RIT score couldn't give.

---

## 4. From scores to a priority list

For each domain compute:

```
Need%      = 100% − Mastery%
PriorityScore = Need% × Weight        (weights from file 01 §3)
```

Sort domains by **PriorityScore**, highest first. That ordered list is the input to the
study-plan agent (file 05). Higher PriorityScore = a domain that is both **important** and
**not yet mastered** — the best use of study time.

---

## 5. Progress-monitoring cadence

| When | What to do |
|------|-----------|
| **Week 0** | Take this diagnostic. Build the baseline profile + first study plan. |
| **Every 1 week** | Short 10-min skill check on the current focus domain (agent generates it). |
| **Every 4–6 weeks** | Re-take a **parallel form** of the full diagnostic (swap in new passages, same question types/weights) to measure real movement. |
| **Next NWEA window (Fall '26)** | Compare MAP Reading RIT; goal is continued upward trend, ideally narrowing the gap to his math percentile. |

**Building a parallel re-test:** keep the four mission *types* (editor / reviewer /
detective / campaign), their point values, and the domain mapping identical; only swap in
new texts — a different article to fix, a new story to review, a fresh case, a new debate.
This keeps results comparable across time while staying unpredictable. The study-plan agent
(file 05) can generate parallel mission packs on request.

## 6. Honesty & limits (read this)

- One test is a **snapshot**, influenced by mood, sleep, and the specific passages. Trust
  *patterns across re-tests*, not a single number.
- These passages are original and **not** calibrated against a national sample like NWEA,
  so the % mastery is a **criterion** measure ("did he master *this* skill?"), not a
  percentile. Use NWEA for percentile, use this for *what to teach*.
- If a domain looks weak, confirm it appears in **two** assessments before treating it as a
  real gap — don't over-correct on noise.
