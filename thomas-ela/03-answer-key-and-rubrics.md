# 03 · Scoring Guide — Mission Pack (Answer Key, Rubrics & Domain Map)

For the scorer. Total = **50 points** + observed fluency. These are **performance tasks**,
so most items are judged with the rubrics below rather than a single "right answer." When
torn between two scores, take the **lower** and jot why — honest data helps Thomas more
than generous data.

---

## 🗞️ MISSION 1 — The Newsroom Fix-Up (12 pts)

### 1A · Find & fix errors `[Conventions · 5]` — 1 pt per correct fix, up to 5

The planted errors (full list — he needs any 5):

| # | Wrong | Correct fix | Type |
|---|-------|------------|------|
| 1 | "**the** octopus might be…" | **The** (capitalize sentence start) | Capitalization |
| 2 | "the most amazing thing is **it's** brain" | **its** brain | Its vs it's |
| 3 | "in each of **it's** eight arms" | **its** eight arms | Its vs it's |
| 4 | "make choices on **it's** own" | **its** own | Its vs it's |
| 5 | "experts at disguise **they** can change…" | "…disguise**.** **They** can…" (run-on → split, or use a semicolon) | Run-on sentence |
| 6 | "recognize individual **humans** faces" | **human** faces | Adjective vs plural noun |

> Score 1 pt for each correctly identified **and** corrected error, max 5. Identifying
> without fixing = ½, round down across the section. Bonus awareness (not extra points): a
> sharp editor may also flag the comma-light long sentence — note it as a strength.

### 1B · The sentence that doesn't belong `[Inference & Evidence · 3]`

**Answer:** *"The fastest land animal is the cheetah."* It's about land speed and has nothing
to do with octopuses — it breaks the article's focus.

| Pts | Criteria |
|----|----------|
| 3 | Identifies the cheetah sentence **and** clearly explains it's off-topic/breaks focus. |
| 2 | Identifies it, explanation thin. |
| 1 | Vaguely senses something is off but picks wrong/partly. |
| 0 | Misses it. |

### 1C · Better headline `[Writing · 2]`

Any headline that is **accurate + engaging** (e.g., *"Nine Brains, Three Hearts: Meet the
Ocean's Genius"*). 2 = catchy *and* accurate; 1 = accurate but flat, or catchy but
misleading; 0 = missing/inaccurate.

### 1D · Stronger word choices `[Vocabulary · 2]`

Replaces 2 vague/overused words with precise upgrades, meaning intact. Likely targets:
*amazing → astonishing/remarkable*, *strangest → most bizarre*, *experts → masters*, *tiny
→ miniature*. 1 pt per strong swap.

## ⭐ MISSION 2 — The Story Reviewer (13 pts)

### 2A · Rating + hook — **warm-up, not scored** (gets him into voice)

### 2B · Theme + turning point with evidence `[Literature · 3 + Inference & Evidence · 4]`

**Theme** (Literature, 3): courage means acting *despite* fear; you find strength by
connecting to someone/something you love — not by being perfect. (Accept reasonable
variations supported by the text.)
**Turning point** (Inference & Evidence, up to 4): the moment Priya **"looked up"** and
found her brother Arun — that's when she chooses to play instead of run.

| Trait | Full credit |
|-------|-------------|
| Theme stated (3) | Names an underneath-the-plot idea, not just "a girl plays guitar." |
| Turning point + 2 quotes (4) | Identifies the look-up/Arun moment **and** quotes ≥2 exact details (e.g., *"she looked up,"* *"the exit sign glowed red and kind,"* *"her voice cracked on the high note like ice on a pond"*). 1 pt per accurate, relevant quote; 2 pts for correct turning point. |

### 2C · Figurative language `[Vocabulary · 2]`

Any correct example **with** an explanation of its effect. Examples: *"the microphone
waited like an open mouth"* (simile — makes the mic feel threatening/hungry); *"her fingers
turned to strangers"* (metaphor — she's lost control); *"voice cracked … like ice on a
pond"* (simile — fragile, about to break). 2 = example + effect; 1 = example only; 0 = none.

### 2D · Recommendation `[Writing · 3]`

| Pts | Criteria |
|----|----------|
| 3 | Names **who** should read it and gives a **convincing why** tied to the story, in clear sentences. |
| 2 | Recommendation present but reason generic. |
| 1 | "It's good, read it" with no real reasoning. |
| 0 | Missing. |

## 🔍 MISSION 3 — The Case File (11 pts)

### 3A · Solve the case `[Inference & Evidence · 4]`

**Solution:** The **hamster, Nibbles, got out** (latch hung open), and the model was
destroyed/eaten or knocked apart by the loose hamster — *not* stolen by a person. The window
and only other key are ruled out, so it had to be something already inside the room. The
volcano model (often built from cardboard, baking soda, etc.) was chewed/wrecked; the
reddish-brown ring is dried "lava"/liquid from the model, and Nibbles is quiet/guilty with
an empty water bottle because he was loose and into the project.

| Pts | Criteria |
|----|----------|
| 4 | Concludes it was the loose hamster (no human thief) with sound reasoning. |
| 3 | Right culprit, reasoning a bit thin. |
| 2 | Rules out the humans (window locked, Ms. Lee away) but doesn't land on the hamster. |
| 1 | Guesses without using the locked-room logic. |

> Accept any logically airtight alternative that genuinely fits *all* the clues — reward the
> reasoning, not a memorized answer.

### 3B · Cite the clues `[Inference & Evidence · 3]`

1 pt each for connecting up to three clues to the conclusion: **(a)** window locked + only
other key-holder out of town → no human entry; **(b)** the **open cage latch** + quiet,
"guilty" hamster + empty water bottle → Nibbles was loose; **(c)** the reddish-brown ring +
cracker crumb → the model was disturbed/consumed at the scene.

### 3C · Words from context `[Vocabulary · 2 + 2]`

- **confirmed** = proven true / backed up (clue: "by three relatives" shows her story was
  checked). 2 = meaning + how he knew; 1 = meaning only.
- **secured** = fastened/locked shut (clue: contrast with "hung open," "normally"). 2 =
  meaning + how he knew; 1 = meaning only.

## 📣 MISSION 4 — Make Your Case (14 pts)

### 4A · Plan — **not scored** (scratch space; glance at it to see if he plans before writing)

### 4B · The pitch `[Informational synthesis · 6 + Writing · 7 + Conventions · 2]`

| Strand | Pts | What full credit looks like |
|--------|:--:|------------------------------|
| **Informational synthesis** | 6 | Pulls **accurate** facts from **both** sheets. 3 pts for correctly using Sheet A (sleep need/focus), 3 pts for correctly using Sheet B (bus cost / later dismissal) — *especially* if he addresses the opposing fact instead of ignoring it. |
| **Writing quality** | 7 | Clear position (2); organized claim → reasons → close (2); persuasive, addresses the other side (2); strong closing line (1). |
| **Conventions in real writing** | 2 | Mostly correct sentences, punctuation, capitalization in his own prose (2 = clean; 1 = readable with several slips; 0 = hard to follow). |

> The point of forcing "use even the fact that argues against you" is to measure real
> **synthesis and counter-argument** — a Grade 5–6 stretch skill that separates good writers
> from great ones. Reward it heavily.

## 📖 Observed: Fluency & Stamina (not in the 50)

While he reads any passage, optionally have him read a short stretch aloud and note rate,
expression, and whether focus held through the longer Mission 4 sheets. Rate **1–4** and
record it (×25 → a %) in the tracking table.

## 🎯 Score → Domain map (feeds the diagnosis & study plan)

Sum points per domain, divide by max → **% mastery**. Transfer into file 01's confidence
column and into the study-plan agent (file 05).

| Domain (matches file 01) | Tasks | Max pts |
|--------------------------|-------|:------:|
| **Reading — Inference & Evidence** | 1B, 2B (turning pt), 3A, 3B | **14** |
| **Writing — Constructed Response** | 1C, 2D, 4B (writing strand) | **12** |
| **Vocabulary & Word Study** | 1D, 2C, 3C | **8** |
| **Conventions** | 1A, 4B (conventions strand) | **7** |
| **Reading — Informational Text** | 4B (synthesis strand) | **6** |
| **Reading — Literature** | 2B (theme) | **3** |
| **Fluency & Stamina** | observed | — |
| | **TOTAL** | **50** |

> **Coverage note (honest):** performance tasks naturally load **inference, writing, and
> synthesis** — exactly Thomas's top-weighted growth areas, which is the point. The trade-off
> is that **literature** and **conventions** get fewer dedicated points here than in a
> worksheet. If a re-test needs a cleaner read on those two, add a second short story-review
> mission (literature) and a second editing mission (conventions). The study-plan agent can
> generate either on request.

### Bands

| % Mastery | Meaning | Plan response |
|-----------|---------|---------------|
| 90–100% | Strength — maintain | Light enrichment |
| 70–89% | Solid — polish | Weekly light practice |
| 50–69% | Developing — focus | Primary weekly target |
| < 50% | Priority gap | Front-load, re-teach, re-test sooner |

Compute `PriorityScore = (100% − Mastery%) × Weight` (weights in file 01 §3), sort highest
first, hand to the study-plan agent.
