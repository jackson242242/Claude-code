# PLAYBOOK — Growing @TheDragonHashira (anime Shorts → monetization)

A plain-English guide to the workflow. The two agents do the busywork; this explains the
strategy and how to run them so she can spend her time **creating**, not planning.

## The honest truth about monetizing anime edits (read this first)
This is the hardest niche to monetize directly, and pretending otherwise would waste her time:
- **Content ID:** Anime footage is owned by the studios. When you use it, YouTube can route
  the ad money to *them*, not you — even on a viral video.
- **"Reused content" rule:** A channel that's only raw clip compilations can be **rejected
  from the Partner Program** for not being transformative enough.
- **What fixes both:** Make the video *hers* — a **ranking, a hot take, analysis, a
  "who-would-win," original voiceover/commentary, or a reaction** layered on top of clips.
  This is the single most important habit, and the Content Creator agent enforces it.

So the plan is: **transformative Shorts** to grow + qualify for YPP, **plus diversified
income** so she's not relying on Shorts ad-share alone (which has a low payout):
affiliate links → fan funding → brand/sponsor deals → cross-posting to TikTok & Instagram Reels.

## The growth levers that actually move views/likes/subs
1. **The hook (first 1–2 seconds)** — biggest lever on Shorts. If they don't swipe away, the
   algorithm pushes it. Every brief leads with a tested hook.
2. **Consistency** — a sustainable schedule (e.g. 1/day or 4/week) beats sporadic bursts.
3. **Packaging** — title, first caption line, and thumbnail (for long-form/community).
4. **Reuse the work** — post the same edit to TikTok & IG Reels. Triple the reach, ~zero extra effort.
5. **One experiment a week** — change *one* thing (hook OR title OR thumbnail) and compare.
6. **Engagement** — pinned comment + a question = more comments = more reach.

## The two agents
| Agent | Run it as | What it does |
|-------|-----------|--------------|
| **Kai** — Content Creator | `/content-creator` | Pulls current anime/Shorts trends and outputs a batch of 5–7 ready-to-edit briefs (hook, transformative angle, beats, audio, title/description/hashtags/pinned comment) + Canva thumbnails. |
| **Mara** — Channel Manager | `/channel-manager` | Takes her pasted YouTube Studio numbers, diagnoses what's working, tracks monetization progress + copyright risk, sets the cadence + weekly test, and hands Kai the next priorities. |

They form a **loop:** Mara reads the numbers → tells Kai what to make → Kai makes it →
new numbers → Mara again.

## Weekly operating loop (≈30 min of her time)
1. **Mon — Review:** open YouTube Studio, copy the last week's stats, run `/channel-manager`,
   paste them in. It updates `CHANNEL.md` and writes a review with priorities.
2. **Mon — Plan:** run `/content-creator`. It reads those priorities and fills
   `content-queue.md` with a batch + thumbnails.
3. **Tue–Wed — Batch create:** she edits the whole batch in one or two sittings (briefs are
   tagged `quick`/`standard`/`premium` so she can match her energy).
4. **All week — Publish:** post on the set cadence; cross-post each to TikTok/IG.
5. Repeat. Trends in `CHANNEL.md` show whether it's working.

## Files
- `CHANNEL.md` — source of truth: niche, goals, KPI history, monetization status.
- `content-queue.md` — Kai's output, the briefs she edits from.
- `reviews/` — Mara's dated weekly reviews (wins, problems, "For Kai" priorities).

## What the agents can't do (honest limits)
- They **can't see her analytics automatically** — YouTube blocks that here, so Mara works
  from numbers she pastes. (Ask me about OAuth options if you want to change this.)
- They **can't publish or guarantee view/sub counts** — they prepare and optimize; she posts.
