# Thomas's Money Adventure 💰
### A 4-week interactive investing course for a 9-year-old

**Who it's for:** Thomas, age 9
**Goal:** By the end, Thomas can explain in his own words what investing is, what a
stock is, why compound interest is "magic," and why you don't put all your eggs in
one basket.
**Format:** 6 short missions (15–25 minutes each) in the interactive app
(`index.html` — just open it in any browser, no internet needed), plus one
hands-on offline activity per week. Earn a ⭐ for each completed mission.

---

## How to run the course

1. Open `index.html` in a browser (works on a tablet too).
2. Do roughly **two missions per week** — short and fun beats long and boring.
3. After each mission, ask Thomas to **explain it back to you** in one sentence.
   If he can teach it, he's learned it.
4. Do the weekly offline activity together — that's where it sticks.

---

## Week 1 — Money can grow 🌱

**Mission 1 (app): "Spend, Save, or Plant?"**
Thomas chooses what to do with $10 of birthday money and sees what happens to
each choice over time. Key idea: *investing means putting money to work so it
can grow, instead of just sitting still.*

**Mission 2 (app): "Own a Slice of Pizza Planet 🍕"**
A company is like a giant pizza, and a stock is one slice. Thomas buys slices
of a pretend pizza company and watches his slices become worth more when the
company does well — and less when it doesn't. Key idea: *a stock is a tiny
piece of a real company, and its price follows how the company is doing.*

**Offline activity: The Three Jars.**
Set up three jars: **Spend**, **Save**, **Invest**. Each time Thomas gets
allowance or gift money, he splits it himself (any split he likes — the point
is making the choice on purpose).

**Ask him:** "If you own a slice of a company, do you want the company to do
well or badly? Why?"

---

## Week 2 — The magic of compound interest ✨

**Mission 3 (app): "The Snowball Machine ❄️"**
Sliders for starting money, money added each month, growth rate, and years.
Thomas watches two bars race: a piggy bank (no growth) vs. an invested
snowball. Key idea: *compound interest means your money earns money, and then
THAT money earns money too — like a snowball rolling downhill getting bigger
and bigger.*

**Offline activity: The Doubling Rice Game.**
Day 1: put 1 grain of rice on a plate. Double it every day (2, 4, 8, 16…).
Ask him to guess on day 1 how much rice there will be on day 20
(answer: over 500,000 grains!). Doubling feels slow at first, then explodes —
that's compounding.

**Parent bonus:** Offer "parent interest" on his Save jar — e.g. 10% added at
the end of each month. He'll feel compounding in his own pocket.

**Ask him:** "Why does the snowball grow faster at the END than at the start?"

---

## Week 3 — Risk, and not putting all your eggs in one basket 🧺

**Mission 4 (app): "Eggs & Baskets"**
Thomas invests 100 coins either all in ONE pretend company or spread across
FOUR, then spins the year and sees what happens. Repeat a few spins. Key
ideas: *investments can go DOWN, not just up; spreading money out
(diversification) means one bad surprise can't wipe you out.*

**Offline activity: Company Detective.**
Walk around the house and find 5 things made by companies people can own
stock in (the TV, his sneakers, the cereal box…). Investing stops being
abstract when he realizes companies make the stuff he uses every day.

**Ask him:** "If you had 100 coins, would you put them all in one company or
split them up? What could go wrong each way?"

---

## Week 4 — Put it all together 🏆

**Mission 5 (app): "The Lemonade Stand Stock Market 🍋"**
A 20-day stock-trading game. Thomas starts with $100, buys and sells shares
of a lemonade stand whose price moves with news (heatwave! rain! a rival
stand opens!). Key ideas: *prices wiggle every day; news moves prices; nobody
can predict every wiggle — which is why patience usually beats panic.*

**Mission 6 (app): "The Big Money Quiz"**
8 questions covering the whole course, instant feedback, trophy at the end.

**Offline activity: The Real Watch-List.**
Pick 2–3 companies Thomas actually likes (e.g. Nintendo, Disney, McDonald's).
Once a week, look up the share price together and write it on a chart on the
fridge. No real money needed — watching real prices wiggle for a month
teaches more than any lecture.

---

## The 5 big ideas (cheat sheet)

1. **Investing** = putting money to work so it grows, like planting a seed.
2. **A stock** = a tiny slice of a real company.
3. **Compound interest** = your money earns money, and that money earns money
   too. Time is the secret ingredient — starting young is a superpower.
4. **Risk** = investments go down sometimes. Spread your eggs across baskets.
5. **Patience wins** = prices wiggle daily; long-term thinking beats panic.

## Tips for the grown-up

- Keep sessions under 25 minutes. Stop while it's still fun.
- Let him be "wrong" in the games — losing pretend money is the cheapest
  lesson he'll ever get.
- Use his own numbers: "your $10 allowance," not "$10,000."
- The single most powerful fact for a 9-year-old: **money invested at 9 has
  ~50+ years to snowball.** Time is the one advantage he has more of than
  any adult.
