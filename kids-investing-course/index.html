Investments don't only go UP — sometimes they go DOWN. That's called risk. Smart investors spread their money out. Let's test it! You have 100 coins.

🎉 You discovered diversification! (A fancy word for "spread it out.") With ONE basket, one bad year can smash everything. With FOUR baskets, a winner usually makes up for a loser. Less scary, more steady!

MISSION 9

🔧 Private Equity: Buy → Fix → Sell

Private equity (PE) firms buy a WHOLE company that's a bit sleepy, fix it up to make it better, and sell it later for more money — like flipping an old house. 🏚️➡️🏡

You found the Sleepy Toy Factory worth $100. Clever trick: you pay with $40 of your own money + a $60 loan. (Borrowing to buy is called leverage.)

You have $50 to spend on upgrades. Pick wisely — each one makes the factory worth more!

🛠️ Upgrade budget left: $50
🏭 Factory value now: $100
🎉 You did a buyout! PE firms buy, improve, and sell whole companies. Because they used a loan (leverage), a small win on the factory turned into a BIG win on the $40 they actually risked. (But careful — leverage makes losses bigger too!)

MISSION 10

🏗️ How a Private Equity Fund is Built

One person can't buy a whole factory alone — so PE firms build a fund (a giant shared pot of money). Two kinds of people make it work:

💰 LPs = Limited Partners
The money-givers: pension funds, universities, rich families. They put the cash IN but don't run things.
⬇️ give money to
🏦 THE FUND
One big pot — say $100,000,000
⬇️ is run by
🧑‍💼 GP = General Partner
The money-bosses. They DECIDE which companies to buy, fix & sell. They charge a 2% yearly fee + keep 20% of the profits (called carry).

👆 Press play to watch the money flow and split up.
🧠 Quick check (answer all 3 to win your star):

🎉 You understand fund structure! LPs bring the money, the GP makes the decisions, and profits flow back to the LPs — with the GP keeping a slice called carry. That's how almost every PE fund, and even hedge funds, are built.

MISSION 12

🧑‍💼 Asset Management (Looking After Other People's Money)

An asset manager is trusted to invest money for LOTS of people at once. They build a mix (a "portfolio") and charge a small fee. Different people want different mixes!

Your client, Mr. Penny, hands you $1,000 and says: "Grow my money nicely, but please don't scare me with huge drops." Build his mix (the sliders auto-balance to 100%):

📈 Stocks (big growth, big swings) 50%
🏛️ Bonds (steady, smaller growth) 30%
💵 Cash (super safe, barely grows) 20%

🎉 You're an asset manager! You matched the MIX to what the client wanted. More stocks = more growth but scarier drops; more bonds & cash = calmer but slower. Real asset managers do exactly this for millions of people (that's what mutual funds and ETFs are!).
